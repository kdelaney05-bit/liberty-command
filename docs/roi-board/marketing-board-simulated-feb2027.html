Assumptions · Read Before Showing AnyoneSIM
✓S1
Everything after 16 Aug 2026 is a projection. Baseline: the approved 16 Aug probe walk (243 jobs / $1,248,081 July, 100% source-tagged) and the card-keyed spend book. Apr–Jul are measured months.
~S2
Spend book held flat at $68,672/mo — July's corrected composition (migration 175: six fixed-opex SaaS vendors out of the book, Next Level stays ⚑3), all six months. No budget is added, moved, or cancelled. Brand books: Fencing $34,048 · Oasis $19,819 · Pro-Tec $14,805.
~S3
Each source continues at its own Apr–Jul pace. No growth curve is invented — Google's Apr→Jul climb is deliberately NOT extrapolated. Flat is the conservative read of "keep doing exactly this."
⚑S4
Seasonality is applied Nov–Feb, and the curve is TYPED, not measured. No winter exists in the warehouse (data starts Apr 2026), so these factors are an owner's assumption wearing an assumption's badge: Fencing ×0.75 / 0.55 / 0.50 / 0.65, Oasis ×0.60 / 0.35 / 0.35 / 0.55 (Nov / Dec / Jan / Feb — the Feb uptick is spring booking, signings run ahead of installs), Roofing flat (a referral book, and outside the instruction's scope — its own winter risk is real and unmodeled). Leads and signings scale together; spend does not. The first measured winter replaces this flag with data.
~S5
Close rates, tickets and cycle times held at July's measured values. Speed-to-close stays a process constant (6 days) because it measured as one.
~S6
Jared Heideman departed Aug 2026. His ~26 units/mo redistribute to Ron, Vedder and Haakon at their own measured close rates. Travis's July 8% was a routing artifact (inherited unsigned files) and normalizes — not an improvement claim.
⚑S7
The leaks are kept, on purpose. Angi still returns $3.94/1, Eric still gets the most Google volume at the lowest conversion, 23 Google leads/mo still route to nobody, and the stall compounds to ~2,140 open leads. "Exactly as we are" includes the problems — that is what makes the fixes legible as upside.
✓S8
Why the blank live boards fill in: the lead clock is real from Aug 2026 (no more one-shot backfill dating), so CPL, unit economics, cost-per-unit trending and speed-to-close all become measurable by Feb — the withheld sections go live with data, not with estimates.
⚑S9
Flat spend under a winter curve breaches the floor. $68,672 ÷ December demand = $9.2 back per $1; January $8.9 — both under the $10 mark, printed red on the "Flat Spend vs The Curve" card. The model holds the book flat because that was the instruction; the fix (seasoning paid spend) is an operating decision this page prices but does not make.
