Hurricane Season Roof Checklist

The best time to think about your roof and a storm is before there's a storm. This checklist takes an afternoon, and most of it costs nothing.

Photograph everything now

Walk the outside and shoot your roof from all four sides, plus every ceiling inside. If a storm ever damages your home, before-photos make an insurance claim dramatically smoother.

Trim what hangs over the roof

Overhanging limbs are the most common cause of storm roof damage. If it could reach the roof when it falls, trim it.

Clear gutters and downspouts

A storm drops a lot of water fast. Gutters full of debris send it under the roof edge and down your walls instead of away from the house.

Look up from the ground

From the yard, scan for shingles that look lifted, cracked or out of line. Anything loose before a storm is a hole after one. No ladder — that's our job.

After the storm

Do the same ground-level scan, check ceilings for new stains, and get a professional inspection if anything looks off. And be careful with door-knockers who "happened to be in the area" — work with a local, licensed contractor you can call back next year.
