Watering New Plants — The First 60 Days

Here's the secret nobody tells you: how a new plant does in its first 60 days decides how it does for years. And the whole game is watering.

Why new plants are different

A just-planted palm or shrub is living out of the small root ball it arrived with. Until its roots reach into your soil, it can't find water on its own — even in a yard that gets irrigation.

Deep beats daily

A slow, thorough soak that reaches the whole root ball does more than a quick daily sprinkle that wets the surface. Deep watering teaches roots to grow down, and that's what makes a plant tough later.

Morning is the right time

Water in the morning and the plant carries it through the heat of the day. Evening watering leaves foliage damp overnight — which Florida fungus loves.

Read the plant

A little midday droop in summer heat is normal. Drooping at sunrise means thirsty. Yellowing lower leaves with soggy soil means the opposite — ease up.

💡 Keep the mulch ring. The mulch basin we leave around new plantings isn't decoration — it holds water over the root ball where the plant can actually use it.
