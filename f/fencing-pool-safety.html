Fence Safety for Pools & Pets

A fence is the part of pool and pet safety that works even when nobody is watching. Here's what matters when the fence has a real job to do — and what we build for.

If you have a pool

Florida requires a barrier

Florida's Residential Swimming Pool Safety Act requires new residential pools to have a safety barrier. A properly built fence is the most common way to meet it — and the one that also looks like part of your yard.

What a pool-smart fence looks like

Tall enough that it can't be stepped over. Pickets spaced so a small child can't squeeze through. No horizontal rails on the outside that work like a ladder. Gates that open away from the pool and close and latch themselves, with the latch up high.

💡 The gate is the whole game. Most pool incidents involve a gate that was propped open or didn't latch. Self-closing, self-latching hardware isn't an upgrade — it's the point.

If you have pets

Diggers

For dogs that tunnel, the fix is at the bottom: tight ground clearance and a straight, well-set bottom rail. PVC privacy panels leave no gap to start on.

Jumpers & climbers

Height plus a smooth face. A 6-foot privacy panel gives an athletic dog nothing to grip and nothing to see chase-worthy on the other side — half the jumping urge is visual.

Small escape artists

Picket spacing matters for small breeds the same way it does for toddlers. Aluminum styles come with narrow spacing options built in.
