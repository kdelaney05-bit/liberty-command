Plants That Love Florida Shade

Got a spot the sun barely reaches — under oaks, along a north wall, beside the lanai? That's not a problem corner. Some of the best-looking plants in Florida actually prefer it there. Here are the ones we reach for again and again.

Our shade favorites

Cast Iron Plant

The name says it. Deep-green leaves, thrives in dense shade where almost nothing else will, and shrugs off dry spells once established.

Ferns — Foxtail & Macho

Full, feathery texture that fills shady beds fast. Macho ferns get big and dramatic; foxtails stay tidy and sculptural.

Caladiums

Shade is where their color turns on — whites, pinks and reds that brighten dark corners better than flowers do.

Crotons (dappled shade)

Bold striped color that handles filtered light under trees. A little morning sun deepens the reds and golds.

Peace Lily & Ti Plant

Tropical looks for covered entries and lanai beds — lush foliage, easy care, happy out of direct sun.

💡 The trick with shade: layer it. Tall ferns at the back, color from caladiums or ti plants in the middle, cast iron plant holding the low edge. Shade beds read as designed, not dark.

What about grass?

Turf struggles in deep shade — that's why bare patches keep coming back under trees. Often the better move is to stop fighting it: a bed of shade-lovers with mulch or rock looks intentional and stays that way.
