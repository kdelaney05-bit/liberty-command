Get the Most Out of Your Fence

A well-built fence should serve you for a very long time. A few easy habits make the difference between a fence that ages gracefully and one that fights you.

Start with the gate

The gate is the only part of a fence with moving parts, and it works every day. Once a year, snug the hinge hardware and check the latch still catches cleanly — two minutes that prevent the most common fence repair.

Aim the sprinklers away

Constant overspray stains PVC, spots aluminum and soaks wood. If a head is painting your fence twice a day, turn it a few degrees — your fence and your water bill both win.

Give it breathing room

Vines, leaning firewood and beds piled against the pickets hold moisture on the fence. Keep a little gap and air does the maintenance for you.

Walk the line after storms

After a blow, walk your fence line. A slightly leaning post or lifted panel is a quick fix caught early — and a bigger one after the next storm.

Trim what hangs over

Overhanging limbs are the number-one cause of fence damage in a storm. If a branch could reach the fence when it falls, it's worth trimming.
